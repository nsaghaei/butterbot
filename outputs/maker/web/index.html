 MEET THE MAKER

A choice-only brain meets a creative tool.

Milo walks to the printer. The creator asks small questions about function, form, size, material, and detail. Laya answers with choices, never free text. The creator turns the accumulated brief into real Three.js construction code.

The engine validates that code in an isolated runtime, checks geometry and supported behavior, then brings it into the world. Materials change mass, friction, bounce, and soft-body stiffness. The actor collects, places, and uses the result.

Seven physical behaviors

Rigid props, collision-aware characters, suspended-wheel vehicles, deformable volumes, hanging ropes, pinned cloth, and breakable assemblies. New creations stay in the garden between objectives.

Two solvers, one visible scene

Rapier drives rigid bodies, vehicles, and character controllers. Ammo simulates soft bodies against the same floor and live collision copies of Rapier objects. Soft reaction forces do not push Rapier objects back. Colliders and fracture pieces are bounded approximations.

Generated shapes can be novel. Working behavior comes from validated engine contracts. Sample/test modes are labeled and are never reported as real model generation.
